Till Arne Meissner

Research Fellow · Department of Geography & Environment, LSE

I am a development economist and Research Fellow at the London School of Economics and Political Science (LSE). My research sits at the intersection of geospatial and remote sensing methods, causal inference, and development economics, with a focus on sustainable development: global value chains, foreign direct investment, special economic zones, artisanal mining, and climate adaptation.

I completed my PhD in Economic Geography at LSE in 2025, supervised by Professor Riccardo Crescenzi and Professor Andrés Rodríguez-Pose and funded by a Doctoral Training Partnership scholarship from the Economic and Social Research Council (ESRC). My dissertation, “Essays on Global Value Chains and Economic Development”, studies the local impacts of global value chain participation in Africa using firm-level, household, geospatial and satellite data. Its papers cover GVCs and firm upgrading in Africa (revise and resubmit, Journal of African Economies), special economic zones and local economic development in Ethiopia, and FDI, employment and gender equality in Ethiopia. I also hold an MSc in Local Economic Development from LSE (Distinction, Best Overall Performance Prize) and a BA in Philosophy & Economics from the University of Bayreuth, with exchange semesters at the Université Paris 1 Panthéon-Sorbonne and Kangwon National University in South Korea.

Current research. I work on an International Growth Centre (IGC)-funded project on green foreign direct investment in Africa (with Juan Alvarez-Vilanova, Riccardo Crescenzi, Oliver Harman, James Hayward and Christian Lippitsch): we use large language models to classify around 12,000 greenfield FDI projects against the EU and Rwandan green taxonomies, and find that green investment is larger, and more concentrated in manufacturing, than standard statistics suggest (IGC blog). In “Golden Traps? Self-Reinforcing Dynamics of Artisanal Mining” (with Sreevidya Ayyar, Hemawathy Balarama and Yoshiki Wiskamp), we study the shift from agriculture to artisanal gold mining in Ghana, which has caused severe land degradation, water pollution and forest loss. Combining remote-sensing data and administrative records, we map the spread of mining and estimate a structural model of land-use decisions to understand how environmental externalities, market forces and community characteristics drive the transition, and how policy can steer it towards socially optimal land use. I also contribute to the STEG project “Migration in the Face of Climate Change: Assessing the Potential of Ultra-Poor Graduation Programs”, linking climate data processed in Google Earth Engine to household surveys from Upper Egypt to analyse agricultural households’ adaptation strategies.

Teaching. I am a Teaching Assistant for Business in Society in the MSc in Finance at ESADE Business School in Barcelona, leading seminars on CSR, sustainability, and the financial sector’s role in financing sustainable investment and social enterprises. At LSE, I taught Contemporary Europe (GY103) and Introduction to Geographical Research (GY140), and received LSE Class Teacher Awards in 2022, 2024 and 2025.

Experience. Before my PhD, I spent several years in international development. At Scio Network, I led a feasibility study on rigorous impact-evaluation designs for GIZ’s “Migration and Diaspora” programme and supported the GIZ Results Measurement Project. At GFA Consulting Group, I worked on upgrading textile and garment supply chains in Ethiopia and on an EU-funded trade facilitation project for the Southern African Development Community (SADC). At GIZ, I contributed to the design and early implementation of WIDU, a digital platform leveraging diaspora remittances for business financing in Cameroon and Ghana, and supported women’s entrepreneurship programmes in India. At the African Center for Economic Transformation (ACET) in Accra, I applied the African Transformation Index methodology to 30 African economies for the African Transformation Report 2017.

Technical skills: R, Stata, Python, Google Earth Engine, QGIS, ArcGIS

Languages: German (native), English (bilingual), French (C1), Spanish (B2)
