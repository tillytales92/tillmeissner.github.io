I am a development economist and Research Officer at the [London School of Economics and Political Science (LSE)](https://www.lse.ac.uk). My research sits at the intersection of **geospatial and remote sensing methods, causal inference, and development economics**, with a focus on African markets: global value chains, foreign direct investment, special economic zones, artisanal mining, and climate adaptation.

I completed my **PhD in Economic Geography** at LSE in 2025, funded by a Doctoral Training Partnership scholarship from the **Economic and Social Research Council (ESRC)**. My dissertation, _"Global Value Chains and Economic Development"_, studies the local impacts of global value chains using firm-level, household, geospatial and satellite data. I also hold an **MSc in Local Economic Development** from LSE (Distinction, Best Overall Performance Prize) and a **BA in Philosophy & Economics** from the University of Bayreuth, with exchange semesters at the Université Paris 1 Panthéon-Sorbonne and Kangwon National University in South Korea.

**Current research.** I work on an [International Growth Centre (IGC)](https://www.theigc.org/)-funded project on **green foreign direct investment in Africa**: we use large language models to classify around 12,000 greenfield FDI projects against the EU and Rwandan green taxonomies, and find that green investment is larger, and more concentrated in manufacturing, than standard statistics suggest ([IGC blog](https://www.theigc.org/blogs/green-foreign-direct-investment-africa)). A second project studies the shift from agriculture to **illegal artisanal gold mining in Ghana**, which has caused severe land degradation, water pollution and forest loss. Combining remote-sensing data and administrative records, we map the spread of mining and estimate a structural model of land-use decisions to understand how environmental externalities, market forces and community characteristics drive the transition, and how policy can steer it towards socially optimal land use. I am also evaluating the local economic impacts of **special economic zones**, using nighttime lights and matching methods. Previously, I contributed to the [STEG](https://grp.cepr.org/steg/) project _"Migration in the Face of Climate Change: Assessing the Potential of Ultra-Poor Graduation Programs"_, linking climate data processed in Google Earth Engine to household surveys from Upper Egypt to analyse agricultural households' adaptation strategies.

**Experience.** Before my PhD, I spent five years in international development. At **GFA Consulting Group**, I worked on upgrading textile and garment supply chains in Ethiopia and on an EU-funded trade facilitation project for the Southern African Development Community (SADC). At **GIZ**, I contributed to the design and early implementation of [WIDU](https://widu.africa/), a digital platform leveraging diaspora remittances for business financing in Cameroon and Ghana, and supported women's entrepreneurship programmes in India. At the **African Center for Economic Transformation (ACET)** in Accra, I applied the African Transformation Index methodology to 30 African economies for the African Transformation Report 2017. At LSE, I have taught undergraduate geography courses and received Class Teacher Awards in 2022, 2024 and 2025.

**Technical skills:** R, Stata, Google Earth Engine, QGIS, ArcGIS
